    Why this is the mechanic

    Glover's phrase for it on Sep 22: assets are "expenses waiting to happen." Everything on the midterm's problem one is this loop: a transaction changes two or more accounts, the balance sheet absorbs the stock, the income statement records the flow through the retained earnings column, and the cash flow statement explains the one account that is not an accrual. If you can see the three statements move together, you can rebuild any of them from the other two, which is what the cash flow problem asks you to do.

    Pick the situation. You get the entry in balance sheet equation form, the statement effects, the example he used in class, and the trap.

    How to work these for the exam

    • Set up the accounting equation table first (columns: each balance sheet account, then a Rev/Exp column), post every transaction as a row, total it, then lift the statements off the totals. That is the PS3 solution layout and what he said you will do in Excel.
    • For any balance sheet account, EB = BB + increases − decreases. Three known pieces give you the fourth. Capex, collections, purchases and loan repayments all fall out of this.
    • Indirect CFO: NI, add back non-cash expenses, remove gains and add losses, then current operating assets with the sign flipped and current operating liabilities with the sign kept. His anchor: one credit sale you have not collected.
    • Proceeds from a PP&E sale go to investing. The gain or loss gets pulled out of operating so it is not counted twice.
